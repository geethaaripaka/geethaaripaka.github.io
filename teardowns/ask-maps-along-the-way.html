🧭 Case Study #1 · Along the Way Feature Proposal

Along the Way: Turning saved places into visits

Saving a place takes one tap. Remembering it at the right moment takes luck. This is a feature proposal for Google Maps that brings your saved places back to you when you are already passing them.

Google Maps Ask Maps Consumer Product Personalization Proactive UX

Problem

Saving is easy. Recall is not.

I save places from reels and friends' messages, then forget them when I'm actually nearby. I started prototyping a fix as a separate app, then realised Maps already owns the saves, the route and my location.

Of the roughly 125 restaurants I've saved, I might have visited around 18 to 20.

Maps has made capture almost frictionless: a save button on every place, a default "Want to go" list, and now Gemini reading your screenshots for you. But every one of those features ends at the list. The list then waits for you to open it, at the right time, in the right neighbourhood, with the right amount of free time. That rarely happens.

📱
The reel saver

Saves five or six places a week from Instagram and friends' recommendations. Her "Want to go" list is long and growing. She has visited very few of them, not because she lost interest, but because she never remembers them when she is nearby.

Workaround: Scrolls her saved list before a weekend plan, if she remembers to. On a regular weekday, the list is invisible.
🚗
The commuter couple

Drive the same route home every evening. There is a bakery they both saved months ago, four minutes off that route. They have driven past the turn hundreds of times.

Workaround: None. The place only comes up again when one of them sees another reel about it.
🧳
The trip planner

Builds a list of 30 places before a trip. During the trip, she plans each day around the big sights and only realises on the flight home that she walked right past three places on her list.

Workaround: Keeps the list open on the map and checks the pins manually, which works only when she remembers to look.
The same thread runs through all three: the intent was captured, the moment was right, and nothing connected the two.

Key Insight

The best moment to recall a saved place is when you are already passing it. That moment cannot be planned, so the user can never ask for it. Recall has to be pushed, not pulled. That is the one thing a conversational assistant like Ask Maps, by design, does not do.

The Real Gap

The problem everyone is missing: recall, not discovery

Almost every place product today solves the same problem: where should I go? Pick a neighbourhood, pick a vibe, and get a list of restaurants and cafés. Food apps, city guide pages, Instagram curators and now Ask Maps all compete to suggest the next new place.

But I don't have a shortage of suggestions. I already have a list of places I chose myself, and I never go to them. Nobody is building for that.

Discovery (crowded)

"Suggest somewhere good near here."

  • Starts from the whole city
  • User has to ask, every time
  • Every app competes on it

Recall (empty)

"Remind me of the place I already picked."

  • Starts from places I already chose
  • Comes to me at the right moment
  • Almost no one is building it

Landscape

What Maps already does, and where it stops

Maps has added a lot here recently, most notably Ask Maps, a Gemini-powered conversational layer launched in March 2026. Each existing feature is good at its own job. The gap sits between them.

FeatureWhat it does wellWhere it stops
Saved lists
Want to go, Favourites, custom		 One-tap save, notes, shared lists. Saved places show as icons on the map. You only see the icons if you happen to be looking at that part of the map.
Screenshot to save Gemini finds place names in your screenshots and offers to save them to a list. Makes capture even easier, which makes the recall problem bigger.
Ask Maps Answers complex questions conversationally, personalized by places you have searched for or saved. Can suggest stops along a route. Pull only. It helps when you think to ask, and most people never think to ask about their own list.
Navigation Knows your route, mode, ETA and when you are stopped or moving. Doesn't connect any of that to the places you said you wanted to go.

Third-party apps already try "notify me near a saved place", but they have to rebuild your list from scratch and run their own background location. Maps already has both.

Feature

Along the Way: built for the drive

Along the Way connects two things Maps already knows, your saved places and your current route, and surfaces at most one relevant place at a moment when acting on it is easy.

Surface 1

Before you drive: route preview nudge Before you start, if a saved place is open and within a small detour, the route sheet shows one card with "Add stop". The decision happens while parked.

Surface 2

While driving: voice only At a safe moment, like a red light, Maps asks one short question: "A café you saved is 3 minutes ahead. Want to stop?" Say "Yes" and the stop is added. No buttons, no cards.

Surface 3

Ask Maps prompt A new suggested prompt in Ask Maps: "Anything from my lists on the way?" It answers from your saves first, with context like your own notes.

Surface 4

Trip recap After a trip: "You passed 2 saved places today." Quick actions to keep, remove or plan for next time, which keeps lists fresh.

Design Decisions

The user controls every reminder

How often, how far, how you hear about it, and whether a place comes back are all the user's choice. Maps only sets quiet defaults for people who never open settings.

🔔 You choose whether to be nudged
DecisionOff by default. Users turn it on list by list, for example "Want to go" on and "Work" off.
ReasonLocation-based reminders should never be a surprise. Opting in is what makes "Maps noticed where I am" feel helpful instead of watched.
TradeoffOpt-in limits reach. The bet is that a smaller, happy audience proves the value first.
📅 You choose how often
Decision1, 3 or 5 nudges a week, or every trip. Default is 3 a week, and never more than one per trip whatever the setting.
ReasonSome people want a nudge on every drive, others once a week. A single fixed frequency would annoy one group or bore the other.
TradeoffUsers with long lists will miss some good matches. The trip recap catches the rest.
⏱️ You choose how far
DecisionMaximum detour of 2, 5, 10 or 15 minutes of added travel time. Default is 5 minutes.
ReasonA five-minute detour is nothing on a free evening and too much on a school run. Measuring in time, not distance, matches what the user actually spends.
TradeoffEvery trip needs a detour calculation instead of a simple radius check. Worth it, because a wrong "it's close" breaks trust faster than a missed nudge.
🔊 You choose how you hear about it
DecisionWhile driving: a short voice question, or off. When parked: a push notification, on or off. Pop-ups while the car is moving are never an option.
ReasonVoice keeps eyes on the road, and the question only comes when the car is stopped or crawling. Safety is the one thing that is not left to the user.
TradeoffSome users will ask for on-screen alerts while driving. The answer stays no.
🔁 You choose whether a place comes back
DecisionAfter "Not now": next trip, after 30 days, or never. Default is 30 days. After three skips, Maps stops suggesting that place, but it stays on the list.
ReasonGetting asked about the same café every week after saying no is the fastest way to lose the user. A skip is a signal, not a reset.
TradeoffA place skipped on a busy day may not come back when the user would have said yes. It still shows in the trip recap.
🟢 Maps only suggests what makes sense now
DecisionNo closed places, no breakfast spot at 10 pm, nothing already visited. Every nudge says why it appeared, with a one-tap way to stop it.
ReasonWhatever the user chooses, a nudge to a closed café wastes their trust. This is where Maps' knowledge of places earns its keep.
TradeoffFewer nudges overall, and opening-hours data is imperfect, so a few wrong ones will still slip through.

Metrics

How I'd measure success as PM

⭐ North Star

Saved-place visit rate: % of saved places visited within 90 days of saving

It measures the exact gap this feature closes: intent captured, intent acted on. Visits are inferred the same way Maps already infers place visits for Timeline, with the user's permission.

Hypothesis: for opted-in users, the 90-day visit rate goes from roughly 10% to 15 to 20%.

📈 Adoption
  • % of eligible users who turn it on for at least one list
  • % of users who use the Ask Maps "on the way" prompt
🔄 Engagement
  • Nudge accept rate (added stop or tapped through)
  • Detour completion: added stops that were actually reached
  • Recap actions per week (visited, removed, planned)
🎯 Outcome
  • Reviews and photos added after a nudged visit
  • Save rate going up after users see saves turn into visits
🚨 Guardrails
  • Feature opt-out rate and "Not now" streaks
  • Maps notification opt-outs overall
  • Navigation safety signals: no change in missed turns or rerouting
  • Nudges to places that turned out closed

How I'd test it

An opt-in A/B test, starting with the driving route preview because the user is still parked, then the mid-drive voice prompt once safety signals hold steady. Expand only if saved-place visits go up and opt-outs stay flat.

Assumptions to validate first

This proposal rests on a few untested assumptions: that most saved places go unvisited today, that drives regularly pass near them, that a well-timed nudge is welcome rather than distracting, and that people who accept actually make the stop. The A/B test would check each of these, with opt-out rate as the guardrail: if users switch the feature off, the nudges are wrong, however many visits they drive.

Risks

What could go wrong

Notification fatigueOne annoying week and the user turns off Maps notifications entirely.User-set weekly limit, one per trip, and skipped places stay away (see Design Decisions).
Feels creepy"How did it know I was here?" erodes trust in location features broadly.Opt-in per list, and every nudge says why it appeared (see Design Decisions).
