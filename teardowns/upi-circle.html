💸 PM Teardown #1

UPI Circle: A PM Teardown

How NPCI built digital pocket money for every Indian family, and why every design decision is more deliberate than it looks.

UPI NPCI Fintech Product Thinking Payments Delegated Payments

Problem

The Problem

Financially dependent individuals (children, elderly parents, caregivers) have no way to transact digitally within a trusted relationship. This forces physical cash handovers, creating control risk for the guardian and loss of autonomy for the dependent.

🧒
Child at school

No bank account. Dependent on cash for food and stationery.

Workaround: Parent sends cash or transfers money in real time when child calls mid-school day.
👵
Elderly parent

Avoids UPI entirely due to PIN anxiety and fear of mistakes. Defaults to cash even at hospitals and pharmacies.

Workaround: Family member must be physically present or available on call for every transaction.
🏠
Maid / caregiver

Carries cash on behalf of the family. Zero accountability or spending visibility for the employer.

Workaround: Ad hoc cash handovers with no tracking of what was spent or why.
Single thread across all three: "Let my dependent act independently, while I stay in control without being physically present."

Feature

What Is UPI Circle

UPI Circle lets a primary user (account holder) delegate spending power to a secondary user, without giving them full account access. Think of it as digital pocket money, with controls cash never had.

Mode Who transacts Best for
Full delegation Secondary user transacts within a preset monthly limit Child, elderly parent
Partial delegation Primary user approves and authorizes each transaction Maid, new staff

Live on GPay, PhonePe, Paytm and BHIM since August 2024.

JTBD

Jobs to Be Done

People don't use features, they hire them to do a job. The JTBD framework asks: when [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome].
🧒 Child

"When I'm at school and need to buy lunch, I want to pay independently, so my parents don't have to pick up a call mid-meeting."

👵 Elderly parent

"When I need to pay at a pharmacy or clinic, I want to scan and pay within a preset limit, without needing to remember a PIN or call my son."

🏠 Maid / caregiver

"When I'm out with the child and need to buy something, I want to pay using the primary user's authorization, so I'm never out of pocket for someone else's needs."

Key insight: The subject in every JTBD is the guardian, not the dependent, because UPI Circle is set up and controlled by the delegator. The feature's primary user is the person granting trust, not the person receiving it.

Design Decisions

Design Decisions

Five deliberate decisions, and the tradeoffs behind each one.

🔐 Biometric mandatory for secondary users
Decision Biometric or app password is mandatory for every secondary user before any transaction, not equally enforced for primary users.
Reason Primary users own their device and are trusted by default. Secondary users operate on devices the primary has no control over. Without biometric enforcement, anyone who picks up the secondary user's phone can transact freely, something regular UPI doesn't protect against in a delegated model.
Tradeoff Elderly and low-income users (the exact personas UPI Circle is designed to include) are most likely to have older, non-biometric devices. Good security decision. Incomplete inclusivity solution.
💰 ₹15,000/month spending cap
Decision Monthly spending limit for secondary users is capped at ₹15,000, with a per-transaction cap of ₹5,000.
Reason UPI Circle expands one bank account across up to 5 devices. Each additional device is a new attack surface. The cap limits the blast radius: the maximum financial damage possible if a secondary user's device is stolen or compromised.
Tradeoff Elderly parents managing medical expenses and full-time caregivers handling household needs regularly exceed ₹15,000/month, forcing a cash fallback for the excess. The cap controls fraud but caps genuine utility.
👤 One primary user per secondary user
Decision Each secondary user can be linked to only one primary user at a time. Primary users can have up to 5 secondary users.
Reason If a secondary user could be added to multiple circles simultaneously, there would be no clean way to determine which account was debited for a given transaction, making dispute resolution and liability assignment impossible.
Tradeoff Multi-funder use cases (both parents independently supporting one child) are not natively supported. One parent must transfer money to the other first, adding friction to a legitimate use case.
📋 Two trust gates at setup
Decision Secondary users can only be added via the primary user's contact list or QR scan, never by manually typing a phone number. The primary user must enter their UPI PIN during the setup/linking process itself.
Reason The contact list requirement protects the secondary user. If anyone could type any number, a stranger could unknowingly be enrolled in someone's circle and potentially used as a mule account. The PIN at setup protects the primary user. It confirms the account holder is the one creating the circle, not someone who borrowed or stole their phone.
Tradeoff Remote onboarding is impossible. Adding an elderly parent in another city requires them to generate and share a QR code in person, creating friction for the very use case the feature is designed for.
⏰ 24-hour cool-off period
Decision After linking a new secondary user, transactions are capped at ₹5,000 for the first 24 hours regardless of the full delegation limit.
Reason The highest fraud risk in any delegated system is the moment of onboarding, before the primary user has observed the secondary user transact even once. The cool-off period buys time to catch a mistaken or fraudulent addition before it can be exploited at scale.
Tradeoff Adding an elderly parent right before a hospital visit means they can only transact ₹5,000 in the first 24 hours, which may be insufficient for medical expenses. Security chosen deliberately over convenience at onboarding.

Metrics

How I'd Measure Success as PM

📈 Adoption
  • % of active UPI users with at least one UPI Circle set up, tracked weekly post launch
🔄 Engagement
  • Avg transactions per secondary user per month
  • % of circles with at least one transaction in last 30 days
🎯 Outcome
  • Reduction in P2P transfers from primary to secondary after circle setup (proxy for cash displacement)
  • % of secondary users who were previously unbanked
🚨 Guardrail
  • % of transactions disputed as unauthorized
  • % of circles revoked within 7 days of setup (signals fraud or trust breakdown at onboarding)

What I'd Build Next

Brilliant feature. Three things would make it complete.

1 Biometric-only auth for elderly secondary users
Gap PIN entry was the original barrier keeping elderly users off UPI entirely. Full delegation reintroduces the same friction for the exact persona it was meant to serve.
Fix Replace PIN with face ID or fingerprint authentication for secondary users on biometric-capable devices, making the transaction flow scan-and-pay rather than scan-enter-PIN-pay.
Outcome Higher transaction completion rates among elderly secondary users, reduction in failed or abandoned payments due to PIN anxiety.
2 Dynamic limits for high-trust relationships
Gap ₹15,000/month is insufficient for caregivers managing real household expenses or elderly parents with recurring medical needs, forcing a cash fallback for the excess amount.
Fix A high-trust tier for designated relationship types (parent, spouse) that unlocks higher limits, but only after periodic re-authorization from the primary user via biometric or UPI PIN. Limits auto-revert to the standard cap if re-authorization lapses, protecting against stolen devices and relationship breakdowns.
Outcome Primary users can support dependents with larger genuine expenses entirely through UPI Circle, eliminating cash fallback while keeping fraud exposure controlled through re-authorization.
3 Extend to bill payments and recharges
Gap Caregivers cannot pay electricity bills or mobile recharges for dependents through UPI Circle, still falling back to cash for these needs, which partially defeats the feature's purpose.
Fix Introduce escrow-style settlement for irreversible payment types, funds held briefly before release, to safely enable bill payments without the fraud risk that comes with instant, irreversible transactions.
Outcome Caregivers can manage the full spectrum of dependent needs digitally, completing the financial inclusion promise of UPI Circle.
"UPI Circle is not just a payments feature. It is an answer to a question India's digital payments ecosystem ignored for a long time: what about the people who are trusted, but not account holders? Every design decision is a careful negotiation between inclusion and fraud prevention, autonomy and control, convenience and security. That tension is what makes it one of the most thoughtfully designed features in the UPI ecosystem."
