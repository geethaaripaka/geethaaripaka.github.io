Context

The Problem

Users: Payment Case Investigators.

Cross-border payment investigations were seen as slow, and the assumption was that the investigation itself was the bottleneck.

🗂️
Case history is unstructured and slow to read When reopening a case or looking for similar precedents, investigators must manually dig through raw, unstructured archival records. Reading and interpreting a single case takes 5 to 15 minutes.
🌍
No consistent format across markets Case records from different markets are entered differently: no standard structure, no shared summary format. Comparing or learning from cases across regions is impractical.
🧠
Precedent held in individual memory Resolution logic and similar-case knowledge live with individual investigators, not in the system. It's lost on rotation and hard to transfer.

Insight

The Insight

When I looked closely at the workflow, much of the time went into rebuilding case history. Whenever a case was reopened, reassigned or audited, the investigator had to go through attachments, SWIFT messages, emails, notes and prior communications across different sources before understanding what had already happened. It looked like normal prep work, so nobody treated it as a problem. But it was effectively a tax on every handover.

Discovery

How I Validated It

Before asking for significant technology investment, I wanted to prove this was worth solving.

1

Confirmed the pain was recurring

Reopens, reassignments and audits repeatedly triggered the same manual reconstruction; it wasn't an occasional inconvenience.

2

Built a lightweight prototype

I used a local model and local data so an investigator could enter a Case ID and get a structured summary, timeline, relevant attachments and a workflow view, testing the concept without waiting for enterprise infrastructure.

3

Tested with real investigators

They didn't just say it was helpful; they started asking for more, like how many times a case had been reopened. That was the signal we'd found a genuine workflow problem, not just built an interesting AI demo.

Product

The Solution

1

Summarise button on the Pega home screen

Accessible from the main workspace. No extra navigation, no new screen to learn.

2

Investigator enters a Case ID and clicks Summarise

The system retrieves the full case history for that ID, whether active, resolved or archived.

3

AI reads unstructured case data

Case notes, SWIFT messages, exception fields, attachments and resolution details are processed and returned as structured plain-English output in under 60 seconds.

4

Structured output

A 7-field summary in a fixed format, plus a case timeline, relevant attachments and a workflow view.

Walkthrough

How It Works

1

Investigator opens Pega home screen

The Summarise button sits on the home screen, so no navigation is required. It's accessible at any point in the investigator's workflow.

2

Enters Case ID and clicks Summarise

Single input. The investigator enters the Case ID of any case (active, resolved or archived) they want to review or reference.

3

System retrieves full case history

The system pulls the complete case history for that ID: case notes, SWIFT message fields, exception metadata, attachments and resolution details. No manual copy-paste.

4

AI generates a structured 7-field summary

Output: case overview, parties, exception type, actions taken, resolution, time-to-resolve and reference numbers, in plain English and consistently formatted. Alongside it: a case timeline, relevant attachments and a workflow view.

5

Investigator reviews and acts

The summary is advisory; it doesn't override Pega workflow or auto-populate fields. It's used for case reopens (reconstruct what happened), finding similar cases (scan precedents fast) and cross-market consistency (the same structured format regardless of how the original record was entered).

Delivery

Taking It to DEV

With the validation in hand, I made the case for investment and worked with the engineering team to take it into DEV on HSBC's approved enterprise AI platform using GPT-5. I pushed for a deterministic fallback, so investigators still get a usable output if the AI platform is unavailable.

Lesson

The most expensive problems aren't always in the obvious part of a workflow. Sometimes they're in the work around the core task: handovers, rework and repetition.
